# Bot model v3 — rebuilt against owner play — 2026-08-01

> **Status:** `reference`
>
> What changed in the simulation lab's player model, what it bought, and what
> it did not. v3 exists because owner device recordings showed v2 measuring a
> different game from the one being played — see
> [`2026-08-01-owner-play-calibration.md`](2026-08-01-owner-play-calibration.md),
> which holds the ground truth and the acceptance targets this document is
> scored against.
>
> **v3 still fails those targets by roughly an order of magnitude. Its output
> remains unusable as a claim about difficulty, upgrades or the economy.**
> It is a better instrument, not yet a valid one.

## What was wrong, and what was done about it

| Blind spot | v2 | v3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dive** | `_find_tap` rejected every anchor at or below the Dive threshold — one line that made a third of the verb set unreachable. The bot had never performed a Dive in any batch ever run. | Searched as its own down-forward fan and taken when the descent is wanted. 0.5–0.7 Dives per web attach. |
| **Reel** | Policy written in *fractions* of the meter: engage above `energy_fraction > reel_reserve`, stop at `<= 0.06`. Scaling capacity scaled both sides, so capacity upgrades were bit-for-bit invisible, and the fixed 6% stop meant the meter could never empty. | Policy written in **seconds of continuous pull**, the way the HUD bar reads. Thresholds are absolute and deliberately not derived from capacity. |
| **Anchor classes** | `tools/simulate.gd` referenced none of the five classes; zones 4–8 were measured as if their defining mechanics were ordinary fixed anchors. | The whole attach fan is read and scored by class, faded by a per-tier `class_read`. Timed spans are released on the deadline the `rotten_crack` cue publishes. |

Two diagnostics were added so the model can be caught being wrong in future
without waiting for another set of recordings:

- **`taps_per_second`** — directly comparable to the owner's measured input
  envelope: 6.60/s averaged over a run, 18/s sustained at peak. An input rate
  far outside that means the model is not playing the same game, whatever its
  distance says. (The 4.71/s this document originally cited was a 30 fps
  undercount of a 60 fps recording; corrected in the calibration document.)
- **`class_choice_rate`** — the share of web searches that offered more than
  one anchor class, i.e. the share in which the class preference could change
  anything at all.

right, the verbs are present, the survival is not.

## Three things measured that say "no"

Recording the negatives, because each one was a plausible fix that a
sentence of reasoning would have shipped.

**Path lookahead made it worse and was removed.** The obvious remaining gap
was that the bot never asks "am I about to hit something", so it was given
one: integrate the current motion forward a fraction of a second and test the
path against on-screen obstacle polygons. Acting on it *while attached* cost
13% distance and pushed input to 6.8 taps/s (against the 4.71 figure then
believed to be the owner's; his true run average is 6.60) —
straight-line prediction is simply the wrong model for a pendulum arc, so it
reported a contact on nearly every swing that passed near anything and
interrupted the good swings along with the bad. Restricting it to the
detached state, where ballistic prediction is honest, was **also** net
negative: 10.06 deaths/km against 8.17 without it, because the early attaches
it forced were worse than the ones the ordinary policy would have chosen. The
code is deleted rather than left switchable; a negative result does not earn
a flag.

**The anchor-class preference is wired, correct, and not yet changing
behaviour.** It demonstrably reads the world — 6.8 silk-highway attaches per
run in Web City, and the class contracts fail loudly when the world stops
reporting the class. But the highway share of all attaches is ~18% at novice
against ~20% at expert, despite a `class_read` of 0.20 against 0.95. The
`class_choice_rate` diagnostic says why: **0% of web searches at 5 000 m offer
more than one anchor class, against 37% in Web City.** Where every candidate
is an ordinary fixed anchor a preference has nothing to prefer, and even in
Web City the four-angle fan usually surfaces one usable anchor rather than a
menu. The capability is real; the claim "the bot chooses good anchors" is not
yet earned.

**Both reel tracks are still bit-identical to level 0** — mean, median, p10,
p90, total distance and death count, to the digit. This is now a *stronger*
finding than the audit's, not a repeat of it: v2 could not have shown a
difference, because its thresholds were ratios that cancelled the reservoir
out. v3's thresholds are absolute, so a binding reservoir would show — and it
does not bind. Across every batch, `reel empties` is 0.00 and `time at empty`
0.00 s. The bot reels in short position corrections of a few tenths of a
second against a reservoir holding 2.0 s, so it never approaches the floor.

## Ground truth on the reel meter, and a correction

The claim that the Reel meter never empties was criticised in the upgrade
audit's superseding banner as **circular** — the bot stopped at 6%, so it
could not have emptied it. That criticism was right about the *method* and
wrong to imply the conclusion was therefore false.

The owner's recordings settle it directly. The REEL button carries a cyan
radial depletion meter, so the fill can be read frame by frame. Run
`726dcc65`, 1 459 frames at 30 fps, upgrades L20:

| Measure | Value |
| --- | ---: |
| Median fill | 100% |
| 5th percentile | 86% |
| **Minimum, whole run** | **73%** |
| Frames below 90% | 17 episodes, longest 1.13 s |

**A skilled player at L20 never brings the meter below three-quarters.** The
deepest dip spends about 20 energy — which is 27% of the L20 reservoir and
would still be only 33% of the level-0 one. So the meter does not bind for
this player at either end of the upgrade track, and *"a resource that is never
exhausted cannot be improved by having more of it"* survives contact with
device evidence.

What does **not** survive is the bundle conclusion: the owner reaches ~2 km
unupgraded and well above 5 km upgraded, so upgrades plainly work even though
these two tracks appear not to be where the benefit lives. Silk Reserve's
2.00 s → 2.48 s of continuous reel is real; nothing here shows a player ever
needing the extra 0.48 s.

## What is still missing

> **Superseded 2026-08-01 by `2026-08-01-recovery-gap.md`.** The section below
> names the right symptom and the wrong cause. Measured since: **88–100% of
> deaths occur with an unused Burst or Dive in hand**, and 58–75% happen while
> detached. That is not a route-choice failure — arriving somewhere with no
> options — it is a failure to *respond* when a route goes wrong, with the
> options still held. Read the recovery document instead.


The model dies in 3.6 s at 5 000 m while the owner sustains the same speed for
48 s, and 95% of those deaths are obstacle collisions on `canopy_shutter_*`
and `canopy_hook_*` patterns. The gap is not verbs, not pace, and not input
rate — all three now match. It is **route choice**: which anchor to take so
that the swing that follows clears what is coming, rather than which anchor is
reachable right now. The four-angle fan picks the first reachable anchor and
the swing goes wherever physics sends it.

That is a different kind of model — one that evaluates a candidate anchor by
the arc it produces — and it is the next thing worth building. It is also
where the recovered tap stream becomes useful beyond a rate: the owner's
aim-tap positions relative to his spider are a direct sample of *which anchor a
human picks from where*, and can be fitted rather than guessed.

## Method

- `tools/simulate.gd`, bot model v3, `balanced_baseline`, `classic`, Standard
  difficulty, rescue life on.
- Course seeds rotate independently of the bot-imperfection seed. No run timed
  out in any batch reported here, so every run ended in a death.
- Reel-meter fill read from the cyan ring around the REEL button by sampling
  360 angles at three radii per frame and taking the lit fraction; the 13
  frames where the HUD is hidden during the rescue are excluded rather than
  counted as an empty meter.
- Recordings are the owner's and are not committed.

### Claim provenance (PL-013)

Two instruments meet in this document and they are **not** of equal standing:

| Source | Provenance | Resolution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bot batch statistics | **measured** — in-run counters | 1 tick, exact |
| Reel-meter fill | **measured** — cyan ring arc | 30 fps; brief dips invisible |
| Owner recordings | **measured** — HUD + tap markers | see the calibration doc's table |
| "v3 is closer to how the game is played" | **inferred** — from the two above | only as good as the acceptance targets |

The inference row is where this document has been wrong before. Its earlier
"the remaining gap is route choice" was an inference presented in the register
of a measurement, and it is **superseded** by
[`2026-08-01-recovery-gap.md`](2026-08-01-recovery-gap.md), which measured the
thing directly and found the opposite: the model dies holding an unused escape
88–100% of the time. Right symptom, wrong cause — and the wrongness was
invisible because the sentence did not say it was an inference.

Reproduce:

```bash
godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/simulate.gd -- \
  --runs=40 --skill=expert --course-seeds=8 --max-seconds=180 \
  --start-m=5000 --upgrades=20
```
