# Bot model v3 — rebuilt against the acceptance test

> **Status:** `complete`

## Goal

Close the three structural blind spots the owner-play calibration named, then
score the result against that document's acceptance targets and report the
score honestly whichever way it comes out.

## Scope guard

The player model in `tools/simulate.gd`, plus contracts on the information it
reads. **No physics values, no zone content, no tuning.** The lab is
diagnostic; nothing here changes what the game does.

## Previous-session review

**previous-session review:** the calibration slice established that the bot
dies 27× more often per kilometre than the owner at the same warp, and named
three fixable causes. It deliberately changed no code. This slice is that
change.

## What was built

Three fixes, each traceable to one line or policy in v2:

1. **Dive.** `_find_tap` rejected every anchor at or below the Dive threshold
   — one condition that made a third of the verb set unreachable. Split into
   `_probe_tap(direction, reach, downward)` so both fans use one search with
   the test inverted. The bot now takes 0.5–0.7 Dives per web attach.
2. **Reel in seconds.** The v2 policy compared `energy_fraction` against
   fraction thresholds, so scaling capacity scaled both sides. Rewritten in
   absolute seconds of continuous pull. The thresholds are deliberately *not*
   derived from capacity — a threshold in seconds computed as a share of the
   seconds the reservoir buys cancels the reservoir back out, which is the
   same bug in disguise, and I wrote it that way first and caught it by
   measuring.
3. **Anchor classes.** The attach fan is read whole and scored by class rather
   than taking the first hit, and a rotten or collapsing span is released on
   the deadline the `rotten_crack` cue publishes — the number the HUD is
   driven by.

Plus two diagnostics so the model can be caught being wrong without another
set of recordings: `taps_per_second` (against the owner's measured 4.71) and
`class_choice_rate` (the share of web searches that offered a genuine choice
of anchor class).

| 0 | 1 646.9 m | 1 977.8 m |

The upgrade penalty shrank four-fold. At the owner's own configuration the

**Scored: three of eight acceptance targets. Still failing.** Pace, verbs and
input rate now match the owner; survival is 20× worse. The publication rule
stands and no conclusion is drawn from these runs.

## Three measured negatives, recorded not shipped

- **Path lookahead made it worse.** Acting on a predicted collision while
  attached cost 13% distance and pushed input to 6.8 taps/s — straight-line
  prediction is the wrong model for a pendulum arc. Restricted to the detached
  state where ballistic prediction is honest, it was *still* worse
  (10.06 vs 8.17 deaths/km). Deleted rather than left switchable.
- **The anchor-class preference does not yet change behaviour.** It reads the
  world correctly, but `class_choice_rate` is 0% at 5 000 m and 37% in Web
  City — where every candidate is a fixed anchor there is nothing to prefer.
  The capability is real; the behavioural claim is not earned.
- **Both reel tracks remain bit-identical to level 0**, now measured by a
  policy that *could* have shown a difference. That makes the finding stronger
  than the audit's, not a repeat of it.

## A correction I owed

I called the upgrade audit's "the Reel meter never empties" premise circular.
Right about the method, wrong to imply the conclusion was false. The REEL
button carries a readable radial meter: across a whole 48 s run at L20 the
owner never brings it below **73%**, median 100%. The premise holds; it simply
was not established by the evidence originally offered for it. Corrected in
both the audit and the calibration document.

## 💡 Idea

Two of the three fixes were correct in principle and bought nothing, and I
only know which is which because each was measured separately. The reel
rewrite is right — fractions genuinely could not see capacity — and still
changes no result, because the binding constraint was never the reservoir. The
anchor-class reading is right and changes no result, because the search rarely
offers a choice. **A structural fix and a behavioural improvement are
different claims, and shipping the first while asserting the second is exactly
how the upgrade audit went wrong.** The habit worth keeping: after fixing a
blindness, measure whether the thing it was blind to actually varies.
